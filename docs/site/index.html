Every service said OK. The customer was charged twice.

Online shops are built from small services that pass messages to each other. Sometimes a message arrives twice, or late, or while the customer is changing their mind. Each service still answers "200 OK". StateVerdict makes those accidents happen on purpose, then checks what really matters: is the money right, and is the order right?

Try it: break a checkout

Pick a version of the shop and something to go wrong. StateVerdict will run the order, cause the problem, read the databases and give its verdict.

    Does it catch real bugs?

    To find out, six bugs were planted in the shop, plus three harmless changes that should not raise an alarm. Then the shop's own tests and StateVerdict were run against each one. The predictions were written down before running. Choose a side, then tap a tile to see the change.

    Both caught every bug; StateVerdict raised fewer false alarms. Nine changes are a small sample: this shows better selectivity on this set, not that it is better in general.

    Tested on someone else's app

    To show it is not tuned to its own example, StateVerdict was pointed at a popular open-source shop built by someone else, with a small adapter and no change to its business logic. It found that a rejected order makes money appear out of nothing, and that a repeated message charges the customer twice.

    8 of 8predictions written before the first run came true
    45 of 60randomly generated scenarios broke the app (46 in an earlier run)
    4 of 4failures shrunk to a single cause, each confirmed 10 of 10

    A random scenario usually does several things at once. StateVerdict then removes things one at a time and keeps only what is needed to break the app, checking each step several times. Press the button on a card to watch it shrink.

    How it works

    1. Cause the accident

      A small hook inside each service lets StateVerdict hold, delay or repeat one exact message. The fault is the same every time; the rest of the system runs with real timing. It needs a small hook in each service, so it is not a black-box tester.

    2. Read the databases

      It ignores status codes and logs. It reads what was actually saved: orders, charges, refunds. Every reading is kept as evidence.

    3. Give a verdict

      Business rules decide PASS or FAIL. If the evidence is missing it says INCONCLUSIVE, never PASS. Because timing still varies, each result is repeated 10 times and reported as a count.
